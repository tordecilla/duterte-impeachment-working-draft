Senator-judge interventions

Sen. Alan Peter Cayetano pressed Atty. Jeremy C. Lotoc on the NBI's conclusion that the President, Speaker, and others had been gravely threatened. He asked why a conditional statement-if Vice President Duterte were killed, the three would be killed-would make them feel threatened absent a plan against her. Lotoc said the NBI saw a risk that other groups could exploit a Vice President's death and blame the three; Cayetano countered that law enforcement should instead focus on protecting the Vice President and questioned the causal logic of treating the statement as a threat against the others.

00:56:27 · S0037800:57:02 · S0038100:57:34 · S0038700:57:58 · S0038900:58:46 · S0039101:00:40 · S00415

Sen. Raffy T. Tulfo pressed Atty. Jeremy C. Lotoc on the NBI's attribution of the term "Romanov." He recalled Mayor Sebastian "Baste" Duterte's Maisug-rally reference to the Romanovs and asked who the warning was meant to reach. Lotoc said he remembered it as a warning about what could happen to the First Family and that the NBI could not validate a separate Operation Romanov; in the bureau's investigation, the term began with Mayor Duterte. Tulfo returned after cross-examination to underscore that he understood the warning as one from Mayor Duterte to the First Family, which Lotoc agreed was correct.

01:05:04 · S0044101:06:28 · S0045501:07:53 · S00465

Sen. Erwin Tulfo asked Atty. Jeremy C. Lotoc how the NBI treated Princess Maui's claim of a "Romanov" threat against Vice President Sara Duterte. Lotoc said the bureau checked open sources after hearing it and found that Mayor Sebastian "Baste" Duterte had used the term earlier at a Maisug rally. Tulfo then tested the intelligence value of the claim and the bureau's validation method. Because Princess Maui did not appear to substantiate it, Lotoc said the NBI treated the information as unvalidated; its counter-source search instead indicated that Mayor Duterte's reference had been directed at the President. He ultimately agreed that the claim was not reliable for the Cybercrime Division.

01:12:05 · S0049201:13:18 · S0050001:15:38 · S0050601:16:10 · S00514

Sen. Alan Peter Cayetano cautioned senator-judges against drawing a definite conclusion from Mayor Baste Duterte's Romanov remark. Reading it against the 1986 revolution, he distinguished a warning from an actual threat and said the relation between the word "Romanov" and the alleged Oplan Romanov remained uncertain. He urged equal fairness to the First Family and the Dutertes while each senator-judge assessed the statement.

01:25:27 · S00586

After the Chair recognized him, Sen. Christopher Bong Go examined Atty. Jeremy C. Lotoc's NBI and cybercrime assignments. Lotoc corrected Go's understanding that he had served 20 years, saying he joined the NBI in 2014, and said he became regional director in July 2025. He said he became Cybercrime Division head in February 2023 after work in digital forensics, anti-organized crime and transnational crime, and the Cybercrime Division, and that then-NBI director Medardo J. DeLemos had designated him to the leadership posts. He then questioned the NBI's proof of what occurred at the November 23 online press conference. He asked why reporters and vloggers who attended had not supplied their own sworn affidavits and whether the agents' account instead rested on second-hand interviews. Lotoc said the media participants appeared with counsel, who advised them not to execute affidavits, but they agreed to interviews; he said the interview was recorded in meeting minutes and that the NBI authenticated and transcribed the full video to check the agents' account against what was actually said. He also challenged the NBI's inciting-to-sedition theory, asking how it squared with the Vice President's discouraging supporters from taking to the streets and whether she had done anything that could amount to the offense. Lotoc said actual sedition need not occur if words tend to encourage unlawful action or undermine public order. He said the NBI's finding came from comparing the Vice President's October 18 and November 23 remarks and her later November 26 video, which it regarded as a consistent pattern of statements tending to stir people against the government.

01:28:47 · S0059301:29:27 · S0060401:29:58 · S0060901:30:05 · S0061101:30:21 · S0061301:31:24 · S0062301:32:42 · S0062901:33:13 · S0063501:33:41 · S0063801:34:55 · S0064401:35:21 · S0064901:35:57 · R10F0575794001:36:10 · S00662

Sen. Pia S. Cayetano asked the prosecution, defense, and Atty. Jeremy C. Lotoc for a lay explanation of grave threats: must the intended victim actually feel fear, or can the offense turn on the threatening nature of a statement more generally? She used contextual examples involving neighbors, boxers, police officers, and a senator, and said she would personally regard the last hypothetical as a grave threat. The prosecution said a private complainant and actual fear by the recipient were not required if the accused intended an intimidating effect. The defense maintained that Vice President Duterte's statements were responses in a Q&A, not direct threats to the President, First Lady, or former Speaker. Lotoc said the NBI assessed both the utterance and intent objectively from the surrounding circumstances, including delivery, addressee, relationship, and recipients' reactions; he agreed the recipient need not actually have been intimidated if the intent to intimidate was established. She then asked Lotoc to distinguish a warning from a threat as a legal concept rather than apply it to the case. Lotoc said an event-based or conditional warning can overlap with a conditional threat, so the distinction depends on context. After the Chair underscored factors such as delivery, tone, anger, and capacity, Cayetano summed up the answer as contextual.

01:56:40 · S0086102:08:28 · S0094002:09:14 · S00949

Sen. Raffy T. Tulfo challenged Atty. Jeremy C. Lotoc's basis for filing an inciting-to-sedition case over Vice President Sara Duterte's October and November statements. Lotoc said the NBI assessed whether the speeches could steer people against the government, disturb public peace, or undermine public safety, initially pointing to statements by agencies charged with national security. Tulfo asked whether it was government or public alarm that mattered, then probed the news and social-media comments used as evidence of public concern. He distinguished people merely worrying from supporters calling for action and elicited that reactions in those comments had been factored into the filing. Lotoc said the bureau had seen Facebook comments urging people to storm Malacañang, though those comments were not included in the investigation or authenticated.

02:29:35 · S0115302:30:34 · S0116002:30:58 · S0116402:31:29 · S0116802:31:52 · S0117202:32:23 · S0117702:33:07 · S0119002:33:24 · S0119102:34:05 · S01197

Sen. Loren Legarda examined whether the NBI's affidavit gave a complete account of the circumstances before Vice President Duterte's November 23 press conference. She pointed to its reference to unpleasant circumstances, including events involving Chief of Staff Zuleika Lopez's contempt and detention, a medical emergency, and a planned transfer to the Correctional Institute for Women shortly before the press conference. Lotoc said those matters were considered, along with the October 18 statement, and that the affidavit of transcription and authenticated video supplied the context; he maintained that the circumstances did not alter the NBI's conclusion because the alleged killing instruction predated the emotional situation. Legarda asked why the affidavit itself did not set out fuller factual findings, whether the NBI had investigated related events at Veterans Medical Center, and whether media interviews covered the participants' emotional state and atmosphere. Lotoc said his division focused on the alleged grave threats and inciting to sedition, did not know the complete facts of the other incident, and said those questions were not in the media-interview Q&A. Legarda said the affidavit appeared to offer snapshots rather than the complete context needed to assess the utterances.

02:38:15 · R10F0949584002:38:50 · S0125802:39:30 · S0126802:40:41 · S0130602:43:03 · S0132002:44:12 · S0132702:48:16 · S0135702:49:49 · S01369

Sen. Erwin Tulfo asked why the NBI had characterized Vice President Sara Duterte's statements as inciting to sedition rather than propaganda. Lotoc said the bureau applied the offense's elements, including whether the speeches could disturb the community or undermine public safety and order. He said it also considered the reactions of government agencies and the President, as well as public comments, in assessing what he described as an active national-security threat.

03:06:39 · S01500

Sen. Raffy T. Tulfo asked whether the withdrawal of some Vice President Duterte security personnel could have led her to perceive a threat to her life. He pressed Atty. Jeremy C. Lotoc on who ordered the change, why it occurred, whether protection had been reduced, and whether Duterte sought replacement personnel. Lotoc said news reports indicated that 75 PNP personnel were reassigned to Manila for a peace-and-order need and that about 335 personnel remained; he said the NBI found no apparent criminal violation. Lotoc did not know the usual security complement for a vice president and had not seen reports that Duterte protested the 75-person transfer or sought augmentation, but agreed the change could have shaped the perception of Duterte or her supporters.

04:47:01 · S0218704:47:49 · S0219404:48:45 · S0219704:49:46 · R10G1738677004:51:30 · S0221904:51:44 · S0222104:52:44 · S0222604:52:55 · S02228

Sen. Alan Peter Cayetano drilled Lotoc on whether the NBI had evaluated the November 23 video consistently. After confirming that Lotoc had watched its full two-hour recording once, Cayetano sought an account of every admission against the Vice President's interest. Lotoc repeatedly located admissions in the November 26 video or said he did not know, before identifying the alleged killing instruction as one. Cayetano then tested purported admissions involving the DepEd distribution and the people-power remarks and the principle that an admission should be read in context; he argued that accepting isolated words as admissions required crediting the accompanying accusations and exculpatory statements as well, and pressed why the NBI's evaluation did not do so. He then asked why the alleged threat was referred to Cybercrime rather than anti-terrorism or intelligence channels. He contrasted the electronic medium with a possible assassination plot and argued that, if validated, preventing harm to the President should take priority. Lotoc said the online utterance gave Cybercrime jurisdiction and that validation remained necessary; he did not accept Cayetano's premise that an assassination plot had already been established.
