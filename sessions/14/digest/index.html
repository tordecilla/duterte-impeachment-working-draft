Court rulings and directions

The court elected Escudero as presiding officer by a 12–8 vote. The earlier constitutional objection did not disappear: Pia Cayetano later registered a continuing objection in writing.

00:46:28 · S0011501:27:46 · S00197

Escudero ruled at the outset that conviction requires at least 16 affirmative votes, while leaving parties free to seek judicial relief on that interpretation. He also placed the burden on the prosecution and recognized the respondent’s presumption of innocence.

01:21:26 · S00192

The chair denied the prosecution’s request to read all four articles and enter a separate plea for each. He reasoned that the articles had already been deemed read, the respondent had filed an answer and appeared through counsel, and each article would be read when put to a final vote.

01:45:32 · S0028601:50:02 · S0030001:53:50 · S00301

The prosecution selected the order Article 4, Article 1, Article 3, then Article 2. The chair also recognized the defense’s equal freedom to present its own case when its turn comes.

02:30:37 · S0036802:31:28 · S00374

The court adopted a five-day witness-disclosure period and generally limited each party to one examining lawyer per witness, subject to leave of court. It also ordered the sealed BIR box returned because it had not been subpoenaed and was not yet in the court’s legal custody.

02:33:04 · S00388

The chair ordered process for two NBI personnel. It did not grant the broader bank and AMLC requests on the spot; instead, it ordered service on the defense, memoranda within five days, and later oral argument before deciding whether subpoenas should issue.

02:39:32 · S0040302:48:00 · S0041602:49:58 · S00427

Subpoena requests could be posted, but produced records would not automatically become public or part of the case record until formally offered and admitted. The stated general preference was transparency rather than executive session.

Prosecution position

Luistro framed the case as a public-accountability question affecting ordinary Filipinos’ money, trust, and right to demand responsibility from leaders—not merely as a political contest.

01:56:03 · S0031102:01:19 · S00318

On Article 1, the prosecution alleged misuse of more than ₱612 million in confidential funds of the OVP and DepEd, supported by questionable liquidation documents and receipts bearing names it said government records could not verify. These were opening allegations, not findings made that day.

02:01:55 · S0032002:02:40 · S00324

On Article 2, the prosecution alleged billions of pesos in financial transactions associated with the respondent and her husband and demanded a lawful explanation. The opening did not yet present or authenticate the underlying bank evidence.

02:02:40 · S0032402:03:26 · S0032503:00:01 · S00476

On Article 3, the prosecution alleged bribery and graft through cash or monetary gifts to officials under the respondent at DepEd. On Article 4, it alleged conduct threatening constitutional order, including recorded public statements and threats. Neither set of allegations was proved in this session.

02:04:22 · S0032702:05:25 · S0032902:06:59 · S00334

The prosecution asked the court to evaluate the case by the Constitution and evidence, and later argued that conduct predating the respondent’s present term could remain relevant if its benefits or implications for fitness for office continued into the current term.

04

Defense position

Sison emphasized that removal would displace the electoral mandate of more than 32 million voters and argued that constitutional accountability also binds those prosecuting the case.

02:13:22 · S00356

The defense relied on the Supreme Court’s earlier Duterte v. House of Representatives ruling to argue that impeachment is legal and constitutional, not purely political, and characterized the House committee proceedings as an improper ‘mini-trial’ and fishing expedition.

02:13:22 · S0035602:17:00 · S00359

On confidential funds, the defense asserted that the DBM and Office of the President documented and approved the release, that the COA ruling was not final and remained subject to remedies, and that the notice and decision did not themselves find misuse. Those were defense representations awaiting proof and adversarial testing.

02:21:04 · S00363

The defense stressed that the burden never shifts and that prosecution material must satisfy standards of admissibility, credibility, relevance, materiality, and competence. It objected that the bank and AMLC requests reached outside the present term, invaded privacy, and amounted to an overbroad fishing expedition.

02:21:04 · S0036302:46:31 · S0041502:52:19 · S00441
