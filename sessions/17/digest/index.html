At a glance

  • Acosta's account: Office of the Vice President special disbursing officer Gina F. Acosta testified that she handled four quarterly ₱125 million cash advances and released the cash to security officer Col. Lachica under the Vice President's instruction.
  • The challenge: Prosecution counsel argued that Acosta remained the accountable officer, that transferring the money to an unbonded and non-accountable officer violated the Joint Circular, and that the paper trail did not establish receipt or end use of the cash.
  • The defense: Defense counsel argued that an SDO need not conduct confidential operations or hold security clearance, may rely on a designated security officer's inputs, and may certify after examining and tallying the submitted records.
  • Senator focus: Senator-judges pressed Acosta on fiduciary responsibility, missing or irregular receipts, aliases, the absence of personal verification, the movement of the cash, and whether her reliance on Lachica was sufficient.
  • What's next: The court expected Limuel Ortoño to testify the next day, ordered medical verification of Edward D. Fajarda's condition, subpoenaed three AFP witnesses, and deferred a ruling on the defense's August 12 manifestation.

Witnesses and evidence

Gina F. Acosta, the Office of the Vice President special disbursing officer for confidential funds, testified that the Vice President told her to release the encashed confidential funds to designated security officer Col. Lachica, who would implement the confidential activities.

Prosecution position

The prosecution argued that the Joint Circular barred transferring confidential funds from one accountable officer to another and that Acosta's transfer to an unbonded, non-accountable Lachica was more clearly prohibited.

00:24:43 · S0009700:25:57 · S0010600:29:55 · S0013200:52:14 · S00291

Prosecution counsel maintained that Acosta remained the accountable officer even though Lachica implemented the operations and received the cash.

03:22:50 · S0113703:22:57 · S0113903:23:25 · S0114503:24:18 · S01153

The prosecution argued that the internal paper did not itself state that Lachica received ₱125 million and that the available acknowledgement receipts and other documents did not prove either actual cash receipt by named recipients or the end use of the funds.

Defense position

The defense argued that the Joint Circular did not require an SDO to participate personally in confidential operations, possess operational experience or training, or hold authority to access security information or a security clearance.

02:37:55 · S0074602:38:13 · S0074802:38:38 · S0075202:39:00 · S0075802:39:15 · S00760

Defense counsel elicited that the head of agency designated the security officer to implement operations and argued that the Joint Circular did not prohibit Acosta from using that officer's inputs and guidance in preparing plans, reports, liquidation documents, and certifications.

02:40:21 · S0077202:40:35 · S0077402:40:42 · S0077602:51:10 · S0086302:51:59 · S00869

The defense emphasized that Acosta examined the acknowledgement receipts, tallied the amounts, checked their stated purposes, coordinated with the security officer, and was satisfied from the submitted documents that the funds related to confidential activities.
