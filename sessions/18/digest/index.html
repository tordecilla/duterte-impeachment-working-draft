At a glance

  • Main event: Lemuel G. Ortoño, Assistant Secretary and Assistant Chief of Staff of the Office of the Vice President, testified throughout the sitting about OVP confidential-fund records and audit replies.
  • What the witness said: Ortoño described signing checks, helping draft OVP audit responses, and advancing ₱150,000 of his own money, while repeatedly limiting his personal knowledge of the underlying spending.
  • The dispute: The prosecution argued that the records showed inconsistent explanations and insufficient support; the defense said those conclusions remained unproved and stressed that Ortoño had not prepared many of the documents.
  • What the court did: The court treated Ortoño as a hostile witness, allowed leading but not misleading questions, and sustained objections to several argumentative or inflammatory formulations without deciding the merits.
  • What's next: Defense cross-examination, possible redirect and recross, and senator-judge questions were scheduled for August 26 before any next prosecution witness.

06

Senator-judge interventions

After the Presiding Officer had admonished prosecutors over words such as "scam" and "mastermind," Sen. Alan Peter Cayetano agreed with the ruling and urged both sides and senator-judges to be circumspect with emotionally charged labels. He said that a statement can live on in media even after being withdrawn or struck from the record, shaping public perceptions of the Court and its eventual decision; accusations should come from testimony or be carefully framed. Prosecutor Mae Divinagracia apologized for counsel's zeal and passion while saying the prosecution wanted to call a spade a spade. The Chair said the point would form part of its ruling and that counsel could do so within limits.

04:25:09 · S0153104:29:06 · S0153504:29:31 · S01540
