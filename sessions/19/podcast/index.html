02

The day’s proceedings

The court opened with Presiding Officer Senator Francis Chiz Escudero’s Rule 18 ruling on public comments about the merits of the case. It then heard defense cross-examination of Office of the Vice President official Lemuel Ortoño, senator-judge questions on confidential-fund records and the pending Commission on Audit process, and closing orders for the following week’s witnesses and bank records.

Why Rule 18 was addressed

Defense counsel Sheila Sison asked the Chair to include a public interview that repeated the earlier discussion of the word scam. Prosecutor Amando Virgil Ligutan said that his Facebook post about misleading questions on cross-examination was a general teaching point, not a comment on the case. The Chair said overlapping voices meant the transcript could not settle whether the courtroom exchange concerned leading or misleading questions and said the video would be checked.

04

The Rule 18 direction

Escudero said the immediate August 12 counsel dispute, prior advice to designate panel spokespersons, and his independent findings of seeming Rule 18 violations required prospective, stricter enforcement. He directed senator-judges to keep their examination of evidence under oath and on the record, and strictly enjoined people covered by Rule 18 from public comments on the merits of the pending trial.

05

Cross-examination of Ortoño

The Chair ruled that the Rules of Court allow leading questions on cross-examination. During defense questioning, Lemuel Ortoño testified that 2023 confidential funds were not used for the Office of the Vice President’s socio-economic program itself, but for the safe implementation of its programs.

06

Aliases exchange

In an exchange about names in confidential-fund records, Senator Robin Padilla invoked Ninoy Aquino’s use of an alias. Senator Bam Aquino identified that alias as Marcial Bonifacio, contrasted the historical example with the records under discussion, and asked whether the prosecution was advancing an aliases theory. Prosecutor Mae Sancho Divinagracia answered that it was not.

07

Audit and record questions

Ortoño said the motion for reconsideration in the Commission on Audit matter remained pending and that there was no final finding. Senator Camille Villar asked how the OVP audit response was prepared; Ortoño said he coordinated with the chief of staff, Vice President, special disbursing officer, and security officer before submitting a drafted response for final approval. Senator Ping Lacson asked about the security classification of OVP programs and proposed that the court obtain underlying documents so senator-judges could assess the confidential-fund activities.

08

What the court set next

Senator Migz Zubiri urged a final Commission on Audit finding during the trial; Escudero replied that COA is an independent constitutional commission. Before adjournment, the court arranged subpoenas for Colonels Boransing and Panopio for September 1, set possible additional witnesses for September 2, adopted its earlier limitations for a supplemental subpoena to financial institutions, and heard the defense reserve comments and objections to that subpoena.

09

Adjournment

Senator Bam Aquino asked about possible consequences for failing to follow the Rule 18 decision. The Chair said corresponding guidelines would be issued in a clear table. The court adjourned until 10 a.m. on Tuesday, September 1.
