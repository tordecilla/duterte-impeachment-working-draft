Senator-judge interventions

Sen. Raffy T. Tulfo asked how COA's audits of the OVP compared with other agencies' confidential funds. The COA witness said each agency's use followed its own mandate and that the submitted documents did not show whether listed names were aliases. Tulfo then pursued the OVP disallowance and recovery, but the witness said she had left the office before its issuance and lacked personal knowledge. Turning to the claimed national-security concern, Tulfo asked why receipts for ordinary purchases needed to be concealed; the witness said COA required proof of purchase, noted that the cited older circular had been repealed, and agreed that receipts for snacks, shampoo, or vinegar would not jeopardize security.

Sen. Erwin Tulfo asked whether the Joint Circular required real identities for OVP intelligence-fund recipients, including entries such as Mary Grace Piatos and Mickey Mouse; Atty. Roderick C. Wamil said it was silent. Tulfo also asked about audit timing and a potential Ombudsman referral; Wamil said compliance reviews rest on submitted liquidations, each agency's spending is audited when it liquidates, and an Ombudsman referral requires a final order and noncompliance. Tulfo later flagged a 2026 acknowledgement receipt for a 2022 expenditure; Wamil said COA had included misdated receipts in its notice of suspension. He confirmed that the review was document-based, not physical, and that the OVP was unusual in declining COA's first AOM.

Senate President Sherwin T. Gatchalian examined whether the six OVP audit observation memoranda for missing payment evidence had been cured. Atty. Roderick C. Asked about rewards without evidence of success, including medicine, Wamil said missing proof meant noncompliance and COA could not verify recipients. Sen. Paolo Benigno "Bam" Aquino challenged the view that confidential funds are secret and unregulated, pressing Atty. Roderick C. Wamil on the 2015 rules and the records used to audit them. Wamil distinguished confidential funds for civilian agencies from intelligence funds for uniformed and intelligence personnel; said an official receipt substantiates a purchase, whereas an acknowledgement receipt records payment and is audited only from its face; and said purchases required official receipts.
