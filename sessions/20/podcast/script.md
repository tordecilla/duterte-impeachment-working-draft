## The day's evidence

Two Philippine Army officers testified about certifications for 2023 youth and information activities that Dep-Ed later used in answering C O A questions about confidential funds. Both described their documents as records that Army activities occurred, not certifications of Dep-Ed spending.

## The prosecution's case

Prosecution counsel Reynaldo Robles said Dep-Ed submitted Army certifications to C O A as support for fifteen point five four million pesos reportedly paid as rewards. The prosecution argued that documents about programs conducted by the Army and local governments had been used to justify Dep-Ed spending. Colonel Manaros M. Boransing the Second supervised the First Infantry Division's civil-military activities.

## What Boransing certified

Boransing described Y L S as a longstanding inter-agency Army program. He testified that his division received no Dep-Ed funds for its 2023 Y L S and I E C activities. He said the certification stated no purpose, and he did not know it would be used in Dep-Ed's C O A response. It certified that the activities occurred and that the participating agencies did their part.

## The limits of Boransing's testimony

Questioned by senator-judges, Boransing said his division received no confidential or surveillance information from Dep-Ed. He described its inputs as open-source information about students and available schools. He said he was not an intelligence officer and could not assess whether a standard activity certification established a link among informer information, a successful activity, and a reported reward. He also said he did not know how much Dep-Ed spent on the activities.

## The defense position and burden of proof

The defense agreed to stipulate that the three proposed A F P witnesses would testify that no confidential funds were transferred agency-to-agency for the activities. It refused to stipulate that no confidential information was shared, or that the officers did not know how the documents would be used. Lead defense counsel Sheila C. Sison pointed to language about Dep-Ed collaboration and information. After the prosecution invoked a prima facie presumption of misappropriation, the presiding officer clarified that the burden of proof remained with the prosecution.

## Panopio's account

Colonel Magtangol G. Panopio testified about his December 5, 2023 certification. He said he learned during an October 2024 congressional hearing that Dep-Ed had used it as a liquidation document. That surprised him because it certified Y L S activities, not money or funds. Panopio said information from teachers could combine open-source and confidential material used to plan where activities were needed.

## What Panopio's document did not cover

Panopio maintained that his certification did not address how Dep-Ed funded the activities or whether its spending was proper. He agreed that Y L S and I E C were community-relations activities, not intelligence-gathering operations. He said the reported reward amount had no relation to his document. Senator-judge Panfilo Lacson noted similar language in two Army certifications. Panopio denied coordinating with Colonel Carlos Sangdaan and attributed the resemblance to common military doctrine and reporting formats.
