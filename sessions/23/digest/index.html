Senator-judge interventions

Sen. Vicente C. Sotto III asked whether the respondent's 15 days to comment on the 104 requests for admission would prevent the court from holding hearings or continuing the trial. The Chair said the period was only for the respondent's comments and would not suspend the trial: the prosecution could continue presenting witnesses and conduct direct examination if the respondent was not yet ready to cross-examine while reviewing the admissions and supporting records. Sotto confirmed that the trial would continue. The exchange established that the admissions-response period would not halt the prosecution's presentation.

00:59:45 · S0021701:00:09 · S00219

Sen. Panfilo "Ping" Lacson asked whether the 15-day period for the respondents to comment on the requests for admission could be extended. The Presiding Officer answered that it could not: the rules made the period non-extendable. Respondent's counsel confirmed the point, fixing the deadline for the requested comments.

01:00:47 · S0022501:00:51 · S0022601:00:57 · S00227

Sen. Erwin Tulfo urged the Senate to resolve before a final impeachment vote which members could validly participate, citing an accelerated trial calendar and four colleagues beyond the Senate's control: one abroad on medical leave, one in hiding and two in custody. He asked whether absent senators could vote and warned that treating the 24-seat base as fixed when enough members could not participate could produce an acquittal by default, making impeachment impossible rather than merely difficult. Tulfo later clarified that he was not asking to lower the constitutional threshold: his position was that the voting base remained all 24 senators regardless of how many were available, while the body still had to determine how absent members' votes would be treated. He asked the Court to decide the issue. On detained members, Tulfo said the Sandiganbayan controlled their attendance, but the Senate could ask it to let them attend.

01:01:29 · S0023301:02:52 · S0024101:04:13 · S0024401:39:34 · S0026402:06:06 · S00333

Sen. Pia S. Cayetano said the threshold question needed fuller discussion with the minority floor leader present and rejected the claim that Avellino v. Cuenco reflected a settled Senate position. She said 12 senators had relied on the case to find a quorum but that the minority did not agree. When the proposed appeal of the July 6 threshold ruling was raised, Cayetano questioned whether the Senate could reopen it at all: she recalled the Chair's position that two-thirds of the 24-member Senate meant 16 votes and that a constitutional challenge belonged before the Supreme Court. She also urged a balanced group of amici if the matter proceeded. Cayetano later urged the Senate to ask the Sandiganbayan to allow its detained members to participate and vote, arguing that their participation would avoid changing the rules or recalculating the threshold.

01:34:52 · S0025101:56:55 · S0030902:05:28 · S0033002:07:28 · S00337

Sen. Robinhood "Robin" Padilla said the threshold discussion required the senator-judges to remember their oath to uphold the Constitution. He described the Constitution as a product of people power meant to protect Filipinos' rights and voices, and said it could not be altered or worked around: any change had to follow the constitutional-amendment process. Padilla said he would defend the Constitution throughout his life. The manifestation framed the threshold debate as a question of constitutional fidelity.

01:34:45 · S0025001:36:42 · S00257

Sen. Paolo Benigno "Bam" Aquino said the threshold question was a constitutional-interpretation issue, not a question of the respondent's guilt or innocence. He cautioned against assuming the prosecution sought a lower threshold while the defense sought to maintain it, and asked whether the parties would submit their positions before oral argument. The Presiding Officer said the parties would advocate their respective positions within the law and that hearing both sides would aid the senator-judges. Aquino said he would not press them to announce their positions then, but reiterated that the threshold question did not itself concern guilt or innocence.

He moved to authorize the Presiding Officer, after consultation with the prosecution, defense and senator-judges, to select the amici, provided that those consultations occurred. The motion sought a balanced expert process for the threshold issue.

01:50:06 · S0028701:50:11 · S00288

Sen. Joel Villanueva supported Sen. Francis Pangilinan's motion to give senator-judges time to study the threshold issue. He read Article XI, section 3(6) into the record, stressing that conviction requires the concurrence of two-thirds of all Senate members. Villanueva said the earlier 16-vote position had seemed non-debatable to him, but that the appeal warranted study. He agreed that incarcerated colleagues remained Senate members and said he had supported allowing them to monitor proceedings so they could eventually carry out their constitutional duty. Villanueva asked whether the body would decide the threshold matter by a simple majority after the presentations, emphasizing that the two-thirds requirement differed from an ordinary vote. The manifestation sought clarity on how the threshold question and senators' participation would be resolved.

In a later manifestation, Pangilinan said that if the Senate took the position that detained members should participate, it would have to seek the Sandiganbayan's approval through the ordinary judicial process. He said that course respected the separation of powers and each branch's authority in its own jurisdiction.

02:04:47 · S0032802:10:45 · S0036102:10:47 · S00362

Sen. Raffy T. Tulfo asked whether the impeachment court could allow two senators in Sandiganbayan custody to attend the hearings. The Chair said their request had been referred to the Senate as a legislative body, not the impeachment court, and repeated that the legislative Senate had to decide it when Tulfo pressed whether the court could invite them. The exchange put attendance or meaningful access to the trial at the center of their ability to participate in a final vote.
