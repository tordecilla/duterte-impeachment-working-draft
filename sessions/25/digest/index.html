04

Senator-judge interventions

Sen. Erwin Tulfo asked whether a declarant may have another person prepare a SALN, whether increased net worth, sales, donations, and zero cash on hand could be reflected in a declaration, and how cash on hand differs from cash in bank. Batu answered from personal experience rather than legal expertise: property sales and donations should be reflected, cash on hand meant cash at one's disposal, cash in bank was separately deposited money, and a zero year-end cash declaration was possible. Tulfo later asked whether the Ombudsman repository detects errors or triggers investigations; Batu said it checks form compliance rather than contents, did not review the supplied SALNs' contents, and said fact-finding ordinarily proceeds on requests or complaints.

01:29:26 · S0048001:30:04 · S0048201:32:04 · S0049301:33:55 · S0050103:35:41 · S0113303:36:07 · S0113703:37:23 · S0114303:38:58 · S01149

Sen. Francis Pangilinan asked whether the SALNs produced under subpoena were corrected copies; Batu said the pre-vice-presidential records came from the Mindanao Ombudsman office, and the Chair clarified that the later corrections had been conveyed by letter rather than reflected in the supplied forms. Pangilinan later distinguished the business-interest portion from the personal-property portion of the form and confirmed with Batu that acquisition cost is required in the latter.

Sen. Joel Villanueva asked when a filed SALN could be corrected, whether the Ombudsman verifies discrepancies or completeness, and whether cash on hand and cash in bank could previously be lumped with other assets. Batu said the repository does not itself assess substantive correctness and checks submitted forms against agency compliance lists; she declined a conclusion on lumping but said that, in her own experience, cash on hand and cash in bank had been declared separately even before the 2025 guidance.

Sotto III noted a Civil Service Commission correction period and asked whether foreign-currency deposits are assets that belong in a SALN and whether the repository observes such disclosures. Batu said the office does not examine each declared item. After the parties responded, the Chair read the applicable rule requiring foreign-currency cash, bank deposits, stocks, and similar personal property to be declared in their peso equivalent.

01:51:14 · S0062101:52:00 · S0062201:52:56 · S0062501:54:12 · S0063801:55:16 · S0064301:55:26 · S00644

Sen. Risa Hontiveros asked whether shares are personal property whose acquisition cost must be declared, and later read the Omnibus Rules provisions including investments, cash, negotiable instruments, securities, stocks, and bonds. Batu declined to give a legal conclusion; the prosecution cited Republic Act No. 6713 and maintained that stocks belong in personal property, while the defense deferred legal construction to the senator-judges. Hontiveros later asked whether unlike categories of personal property may be collectively declared; Batu again declined to decide, and the prosecution said the statute's enumeration required individual declaration.

Senate President Sherwin T. Gatchalian asked whether unvalued corporate interests could leave declared net worth understated, when omitted values or missing SALNs could be corrected, where foreign-currency deposits belong, and whether the Ombudsman checks SALN contents against outside records such as SEC data.
