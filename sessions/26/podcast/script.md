## The questions before the Court

The Court held an amici curiae consultation rather than trial proceedings. It asked whether 'all the Members of the Senate' means all 24 senators or only those able to participate and vote; how unavailable senator-judges affect that count; and whether a senator-judge who did not substantially participate may vote on the final question. The four retired jurists were retired Chief Justice Hilario G. Davide Jr., who submitted a written memorandum; retired Chief Justices Artemio V. Panganiban and Reynato S. Puno; and retired Associate Justice Adolfo S. Azcuna.

## Davide's written position

Davide's memorandum read the constitutional denominator as senators qualified to vote, excluding members unable to exercise senatorial powers because of mental, physical, health, or legal restraints. It treated Avelino v. Cuenco as not directly applicable to impeachment, though potentially useful by analogy. On a senator-judge who had not substantially participated, the memorandum left the matter to that senator-judge's conscience and sense of duty.

## Panganiban, Puno, and Azcuna

Panganiban described impeachment as both legal and political, but stressed the responsibilities of an active adjudicator: senators should test documents, hear witnesses, observe their demeanor, hear both sides, and independently evaluate the evidence. He favored counting senators able to vote and said Supreme Court review would require grave abuse of discretion. Puno urged a contextual and functional reading, rather than treating one interpretive school as simply right and another as wrong. He described ordinary Senate impeachment actions as political questions, but identified a narrower due-process boundary when a respondent's right to a fair and impartial tribunal is violated. Azcuna took the opposing denominator view: a senator may be unable to vote yet still belongs in the two-thirds count while remaining a senator, except after death, resignation, or removal. He warned that a variable denominator could be manipulated toward a simple majority when senators are prevented from participating.

## Participation and the denominator

The central exchange concerned who counts toward two-thirds. Senator-Judge Juan Miguel 'Migz' F. Zubiri's treaty example drew Artemio V. Panganiban's distinction between treaty concurrence and an impeachment trial: 13 votes were two-thirds of 20 senators in that earlier example. Senator-Judge Joel Villanueva asked for a rule against both a veto created by absence and a conviction made easier by excluding opponents. Reynato S. Puno said the text supports competing readings and asked how a detained senator could perform the judicial duty; Panganiban focused on whether a senator knows the evidence well enough to judge fairly; and Adolfo S. Azcuna distinguished the ability to vote from membership in the denominator. Senator-Judge Risa Hontiveros tested that dispute with absence, oath-taking, and detention hypotheticals. Panganiban would exclude a nonparticipating senator, Puno said circumstances and the oath's function matter, and Azcuna retained the fixed threshold despite the risk that accountability could sometimes fail. Senator-Judge Panfilo M. Lacson raised the case of senators stranded abroad. Panganiban called the consultation prudent and favored allowing two-thirds of the available senators to act; Azcuna said failure to reach 16 under his view results in acquittal.

## Judicial review and procedural fairness

Senator-Judges Erwin Tulfo and Sherwin T. Gatchalian turned from the denominator to institutional authority: why the Philippine text differs from the United States formulation, who has the final interpretive word, whether unavailable senators create a constitutional problem, and whether the Court could revisit the Presiding Officer's threshold ruling. Azcuna called the supermajority wording a deliberate design to make conviction difficult and said the Supreme Court ultimately decides whether Senate action complies with the Constitution. Puno described ordinary impeachment action as political but identified a due-process limit where the respondent lacks a fair and impartial tribunal. Panganiban said a senator unable to know the evidence should not vote and that a quorum may reconsider a temporary ruling before final judgment. Senator-Judge Francis Pangilinan asked whether changing the threshold during the case would deny either party due process. Panganiban said no rule or vote had yet been implemented; Puno said the Court was still deciding the provision's meaning; and Azcuna called the matter procedural. Senator-Judge Pia S. Cayetano then put Puno's two justiciable examples on the record—unjustifiably denying a senator-judge a seat and denying the respondent a fair trial—and asked Azcuna about pressure or charges that prevent attendance. Azcuna declined to judge the particular circumstances while restating his concern about partisan manipulation.

## Institutional responses and the submitted memorandum

Senator-Judge Vicente C. Sotto III made a manifestation rather than a question: Fr. Ranhilio C. Aquino's voluntary memorandum of law had been received and should enter the Court record. The Chair said it had already been furnished to the parties and senator-judges and uploaded to the Senate website. Senator-Judge Robinhood 'Robin' Padilla asked whether truth and fairness begin with the Court, how Senate and Sandiganbayan authority over detained senators could be reconciled, whether recordings could permit later participation, and whether the Constitution could be changed without ratification. Panganiban agreed that truth and fairness applies to everyone; Puno described a possible reconciliation of jurisdictions, including temporary participation; and Azcuna said the face-to-face rule could be changed while detained senators still count in his denominator. In his own interventions, Presiding Officer Francis 'Chiz' G. Escudero said revisiting his threshold ruling would still lead to an appeal and a final Court vote, and that he had hoped for a Supreme Court answer before that vote. He asked how permanent vacancies, extraordinary absence, and fractional thresholds should be handled. Azcuna said the denominator changes when membership changes and cited ignoring fractions; Puno urged an interpretation that avoids absurdity and injustice and called fractions a policy question for the Court.

## Immediate procedural directions

After the consultation, the Chair moved to administrative matters. The Clerk was directed to serve show-cause orders on certain House prosecution-panel members after trial. The Chair granted subpoenas for Attorney Ariel Ronquillo and Rendell Sopena for September 21 through 23. Subpoenas for Mayor Sebastian Duterte, Attorney Gary Samonte, and Police Major Jerickson Sangalang were set for September 23, subject to a September 22 assessment of the witness schedule and a possible reset.
