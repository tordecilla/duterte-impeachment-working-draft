05

Senator-judge interventions

Presiding Officer Sen. Francis "Chiz" G. Escudero, in his own substantive questions, clarified that the business-interest provision can cover capacities beyond shareholding and that a person identified in the SEC filings was an incorporator, not necessarily a shareholder. He asked how common nondeclaration of dividends was; Del Rosario gave a tentative roughly half-and-half estimate for a single year. Escudero distinguished directors from officers who run daily operations, then compared Vice President Sara Duterte's 2015, 2016, and 2024 SALN entries with SEC records: Del Rosario confirmed her incorporation of City Hall King Chow Foods and Metro City Chow Foods, found no direct entry for her in Great Jolly Times Food or Times Square B Foods, and said the firms described as "formerly" related remained separate legal entities. Asked how a declared interest could coexist with absent direct shareholding records, Del Rosario offered trust or corporate-shareholder control as possibilities. Escudero also examined possible nominees, who would bear responsibility for inaccurate corporate disclosures, and whether the SEC routinely receives private trust or share-transfer instruments; the witness said those instruments were not routinely required. These were questions and clarifications, separate from Escudero's chair rulings and counsel's examination.

Sen. Raffy T. Tulfo asked how the Securities Regulation Code defines securities and asserted that the Constitution restricts a president's or vice president's participation in a company. SEC witness Gerardo F. del Rosario described securities as including shares and investment contracts; the record has no separately attributed answer to Tulfo's final constitutional question. In a later round, Tulfo asked about divestment and SEC reporting guidance, correction of corporate filings, and records showing whether a director voted, signed resolutions, or managed a company after assuming office.
