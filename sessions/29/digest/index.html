Court rulings and directions

The court granted Sen. Erwin Tulfo's appeal by 13 affirmative votes to one negative vote, with six senator-judges not participating. It replaced the July 6 fixed-16 interpretation with a two-thirds calculation based on senators legally and factually capable of participating at the eventual conviction vote. The chair declared that ruling controlling for the proceedings.

The court will decide whether an individual member qualifies on competent and sufficient evidence, then round any fractional two-thirds vote requirement up to the next whole number.

Prosecution position

Defense position

Atty. Michael Wesley Poa argued that the July 6 16-vote ruling had stood for more than two months and that neither the Senate's reconsideration period nor the court-rule period he invoked by analogy supported reopening it at this point in the trial.

He argued that the 24 occupied seats required 16 votes because absence, detention, inhibition and nonparticipation did not create vacancies; he cited Bayan v. Zamora and the Corona impeachment and distinguished Avelino as a quorum case.

05

Senator-judge interventions

Sen. Alan Peter Cayetano argued that changing the conviction threshold during trial risked a challenge to the proceedings and urged that the constitutional question be left to the Supreme Court. He questioned prosecution counsel on how absent senators could later participate, whether the Court could amend its rules to permit electronic participation, and whether it could secure detained senators' attendance. Rep. Jose Manuel ‘Chel’ I. Diokno said the Court could amend its rules, but favored in-person assessment of evidence and said the Sandiganbayan should decide whether detained senators could attend. Cayetano maintained that the impeachment court might be able to compel their attendance, asked why a lower threshold would not risk a mistrial, and proposed leaving a disputed verdict of fewer than 16 conviction votes for Supreme Court review. Diokno answered that Supreme Court review would become ripe only after a verdict vote. Cayetano later noted a pending Supreme Court case and renewed his proposal to allow electronic participation. When a threshold vote became imminent, he manifested that he and some colleagues would not participate, raised a point of order against the Court taking up the issue at that time, and appealed after the Chair rejected the point. The Chair confirmed that sustaining Cayetano's appeal would stop the threshold vote; the Court rejected the appeal, 13–6 with one abstention. Cayetano subsequently did not participate in the separate vote on Sen. Erwin Tulfo's threshold appeal, which the Court granted.
