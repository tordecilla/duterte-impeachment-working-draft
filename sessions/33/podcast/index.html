The day's main witness

The main event was testimony from AMLC Executive Director Attorney Ronel U. Buenaventura, head of its Secretariat, about reports concerning Vice President Sara Duterte, her husband and corporations. He identified subpoenaed records and summaries prepared by analysts and officers that he reviewed and signed. Attorney Mae Sancho Divinagracia conducted direct examination for the prosecution; Attorney Mark C. Vinluan began cross-examination for the defense.

03

What the reports count

Buenaventura explained that covered transactions cross an applicable reporting threshold. For ordinary banking transactions, he described the general threshold as more than five hundred thousand pesos within a banking day. Suspicious reports instead depend on circumstances identified by the reporting institution, regardless of amount. They can concern a whole account or client relationship, and some show no transaction amount. He described both kinds as starting points for AMLC analysis and investigation.

The four-point-four-billion figure

The prosecution's position

For the prosecution, Divinagracia offered the testimony to support allegations of unexplained wealth and incomplete statements of assets, liabilities and net worth, or SALNs. The defense's challenge

Vinluan disputed both the legal route to disclosure and the inferences attached to the records. He argued that Buenaventura lacked personal knowledge of the SALNs and underlying transactions, and challenged language describing the money as dirty or the reports as repeated flags. In cross-examination, he asked whether the totals represented income, where funds originated and whether the transactions were unlawful. Buenaventura said the reports did not identify income, that his testimony rested on the records, and that he had no personal knowledge connecting the spouses' reported transactions to unlawful activities.

08

The confidentiality ruling

Before direct examination, the defense moved to exclude the witness under AMLA confidentiality provisions and cited restrictions on using intelligence reports. Divinagracia opposed exclusion, arguing that confidentiality did not prohibit every disclosure needed for AMLC's work. Presiding Officer Senator Francis "Chiz" G. Escudero denied the motion. He ruled that confidentiality did not create absolute immunity from lawful compulsory process. Buenaventura could identify, authenticate and explain the subpoenaed reports on relevant matters, with in-camera testimony when necessary. The ruling also preserved specific objections about matters such as authentication, hearsay, accuracy and personal knowledge.

Earlier records and foreign currency

Two further court actions defined the examination's reach. Escudero permitted earlier transactions as a net-worth baseline while urging counsel toward the vice-presidential period. Marcos later objected to foreign-currency disclosure. The Chair overruled her, distinguishing testimony about reports lawfully held by AMLC from compelling banks to disclose foreign-currency deposit information. He directed Buenaventura to continue on that basis. The court also allowed AMLC public documents at that stage, while reserving appropriate objections, and later addressed their authenticity absent fraud or palpable mistake.

Corporate transactions

The corporate figures brought another important boundary. Buenaventura read about four point five five billion pesos in reported transactions for fifteen corporations from 2022 through 2025, and about five point three six billion for the longer 2007-to-2025 period. Vinluan challenged relating the full corporate sums to the Vice President. The Chair sustained the objection to adding corporate totals to the spouses' amounts without accounting for differing shareholdings, noting the companies' separate juridical personalities.

Narratives drawn from news

Another dispute concerned the reasons attached to suspicious reports. Buenaventura read narratives referring to graft, drug-trafficking allegations and alleged misuse of confidential funds. Vinluan objected that the prosecution's slides could associate the Vice President with malversation without adequately showing the news-report basis. Pia Cayetano also objected to those narratives and to highlighting gross inflows and outflows more prominently than their components. Escudero clarified the source and purpose of the visual summary before allowing the examination to continue.

How senators weighed suspicion

Senator Alan Peter Cayetano criticized what he described as a circular process: allegations become news, banks attach those accounts to suspicious reports, and AMLC returns the reports to another committee. He urged independent investigation and fairness. Escudero identified institutions' assessment methods as a subject for later Senate oversight.

Repeated movement of the same funds

Cross-examination then focused on repeated reporting. Vinluan used time-deposit examples to question whether moving the same funds could generate a much larger aggregate. Pia Cayetano asked about the difference between a same-account rollover and a transfer to another account, and requested certainty about the rules. Buenaventura recalled that newer guidelines generally spared repeat reporting of simple renewals, but said a transfer to another account generated a separate report. Older practices and implementation differences could still matter. He read a report describing possible monthly rollovers in 2022, while saying overlapping entries were excluded to avoid double counting.

The limits of the witness's answers

Buenaventura repeatedly returned to what the records showed. Reported totals included investments, insurance, loans and transfers, and he could not reconcile them with balances or actual wealth. Escudero's questions also clarified that displayed peso and dollar figures referred to one transaction, not two amounts to add together. Asked about complaints, freeze proceedings or investigation findings outside the produced records, he declined disclosure and cited subpoena limits and restrictions on revealing bank-inquiry proceedings.

Timetable and courtroom strain

The day opened with Senator Raffy T. Tulfo challenging a reported December 16 verdict prediction by the court's spokesperson. Closing concerns about liability and time

In closing manifestations, Pangilinan criticized suggestions of criminal liability for court-authorized disclosure and suggested taking the challenge to the Supreme Court. Senator Vicente C. Sotto III reminded counsel of parliamentary immunity. Hontiveros opposed indirect threats against the media and a chilling effect on essential public coverage. Senator Erwin Tulfo asked whether the defense would get about six hours, comparable to direct examination. Escudero declined to fix equal hours, but promised ample time the next day without undue curtailment or delay. He explained that objections, arguments and rulings also counted within examination time.

What remained unfinished

Buenaventura's cross-examination remained unfinished. The Chair directed the next sitting to begin with continued cross, followed by redirect, recross if any, and senator-judge questions. Trial was adjourned until October 6 at ten in the morning. The defense rejected proposed stipulations for bank, insurance and investment-company witnesses and said it would comment when their testimony was offered.
