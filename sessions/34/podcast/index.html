From transaction reports to bank records

The court completed the testimony of AMLC Executive Director Attorney Ronel U. Buenaventura, then began hearing bank representatives. Defense cross-examination, prosecution redirect, defense recross and questions from senator-judges occupied the AMLC portion. The two bank witnesses who followed were Eunice C. Sumatra and Raquel U. The main questions ran through both stages: what the transaction reports counted, how those figures related to balances and disclosures, and how the bank records would be identified.

The correction and duplicate reports

One prominent exchange concerned a BPI system bug. Buenaventura read a letter seeking deletion of thirteen erroneous covered-transaction reports. The information-sharing report said that, assuming the correction request was accurate, manager's checks procured for Attorney Carpio would total about two point one million pesos, rather than about two point three billion. Buenaventura confirmed that the erroneous two-billion-peso amount was excluded from the tabulation. Senator Vicente C. Sotto the Third asked about validation of the correction. The witness said AMLC had validated and audited BPI's request and granted it, but he could not explain the bank's system glitch. Senator Francis Kiko Pangilinan separately asked about duplicate reports. Buenaventura explained that entries reported under both spouses for jointly held accounts were deducted; eighty-one million pesos was identified in that process.

Corporate reporting and the China-linked remittances

For Cale88 Foods Corporation, Buenaventura confirmed aggregate reported transactions of about one point two five five billion pesos and inflows of about seven hundred seventy-six point six million. He read three suspicious-report entries citing the absence of an underlying legal or trade obligation, purpose or economic justification. He said the inward-remittance entries were unique transactions, distinct from time-deposit rollovers. Senator Joel Villanueva asked what the roughly three hundred nineteen million pesos associated with China actually represented. Buenaventura said the countries in the table came from the remitters' addresses as supplied by reporting institutions, rather than correspondent-bank locations. Identifying the senders' relationship to Cale88 and the purpose of their transfers required further investigation. The witness also said the Cale88 remittances were recorded for the corporation; the records supplied no amount of benefit to Duterte or Carpio.

Questions about foreign companies and financial capacity

Ownership, investments and the rollover question

Pangilinan asked about Carpio's replacement by Pikimong in Cale88's twenty twenty-five ownership filing and proposed looking into that company. Earlier, Escudero asked how banks classify suspicion; Buenaventura described legal criteria, client profiles and transaction patterns guiding their assessments.

AMLC action, resources and confidentiality

Senators Raffy T. Tulfo and Erwin Tulfo pressed Buenaventura about investigation and enforcement. He described prioritization given limited resources, initial analysis, an internal investigation unit, coordination with law enforcement, and possible bank-inquiry applications, freeze orders and case filings. Raffy Tulfo questioned apparent inaction over Cale88; Buenaventura said Court of Appeals bank-inquiry rules prevented disclosure of whether such proceedings had been filed. He also explained that banks cannot tip clients off about covered or suspicious reports. Erwin Tulfo asked whether AMLC would investigate further; the witness said that required internal discussion. Senator Panfilo Ping M. Lacson questioned the probable-cause requirement and recalled a different explanation from a predecessor. Buenaventura maintained that probable cause was required for bank inquiries and freeze orders, and that investigation of unlawful activity and money laundering could proceed in parallel. Lacson also asked about resources and recalled a budget augmentation; Buenaventura said technology upgrades were being implemented. They disagreed over the witness's authority to testify: Lacson called it implied, while Buenaventura called it express.

Carpio's balances and spousal access

Dela Cerna, branch manager of Land Bank's Davao R. Magsaysay branch, said Duterte's two accounts there were closed and Carpio's account ending two seven three zero remained active. What the court changed and what follows

Escudero initially required ordinary document identification for Sumatra. After consulting both sides during recess, he adopted an abbreviated process for Dela Cerna. He distinguished Republic versus Marcos, where custody alone had not authenticated private documents, and relied here on direct bank production under subpoena, documented custody, inspection and court-supervised reproduction to establish provenance, identity, integrity and fidelity of copies. Specific substantial challenges could still support exclusion, limits on entries or testimony from an appropriate custodian during cross-examination or formal offer. The continuing objections were carried forward. The court also supplied the defense with the October sixth BIR summary after an incorrect email address delayed delivery. The remaining bank and insurance witnesses were directed to return on October seventh at ten in the morning. BIR testimony was to follow those witnesses, and the prosecution anticipated a financial analyst to connect the records and disclosures.
