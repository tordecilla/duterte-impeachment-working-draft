Senator-judge interventions

Sen. Raffy T. Tulfo explored money laundering through legitimate, tax-paying businesses and country risks, then asked whether Cale88's exports, receivables, payables, equipment and utility costs matched its reported scale. Cabrera described missing inventories and implausible cash-only transactions, comparing its small plant and equipment with similar exporters. He had only asked prosecutors about Customs records; Diokno said the prosecution lacked time to obtain export data. Returning to Cale88, Tulfo questioned recurring losses alongside large inflows and hiring, missing raw materials and P125,000 paid-up capital. Cabrera allowed outsourced manufacturing theoretically but found no supporting arrangement and described the scale as implausible. He called money entering and leaving without business use a money-laundering symptom, while expressly declining a conclusive attribution. Tulfo contrasted GenCorp's roughly P1.25 million capital and small reported earnings with costly, expanding Jollibee franchises; Cabrera suggested unrecorded equity and cash flows funding expansion, with reduced taxes explaining underreporting. On Metro City Chow, Tulfo asked why deposits exceeded restaurant collections and where the excess originated. Cabrera treated it as unreported revenue but lacked information to identify other sources, saying such disparities were unusual at this scale. Tulfo discussed double books and proposed comparing deposits with sales, potentially involving AMLC. Asked why BIR had not detected the discrepancy, Cabrera said he did not know and lacked investigation-status information.
