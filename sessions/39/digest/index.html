Prosecution position

Lead public prosecutor Rep. Gerville “Jinky Bitrics” R. Luistro requested withdrawal of Articles 1 on confidential funds, 3 on bribery and 4 on threats, leaving the prosecution to pursue only Article 2. She argued that Article 2 evidence on unexplained wealth, SALN violations and prohibited business interests independently warranted removal for culpable violation of the Constitution and betrayal of public trust.

Defense position

Lead defense counsel Atty. Sheila C. Sison expressly said she did not object to withdrawal. She challenged its consequences: the prosecution could not dictate the defense's presentation or replace an opportunity to answer accusations aired in court with public statements. She warned that curtailing defense evidence to hasten judgment would bear the characteristics of a mistrial.

Court rulings and directions

Presiding Officer Sen. Francis "Chiz" G. Escudero confirmed the withdrawal of Articles 1 and 4 with prejudice and Article 3 without prejudice, leaving Article 2 for trial. The bar on Articles 1 and 4 covered substantially the same constitutional accusations against the respondent during the same term and the 20th Congress; other legally governed proceedings remained available. Article 3 had no prosecution evidentiary case presented under it.

The court directed the respondent's evidence-in-chief toward Article 2 and allowed her to adopt relevant, otherwise admissible evidence already in the official record. 04

Senator-judge interventions

Sen. Alan Peter Cayetano questioned how withdrawing articles after presenting their evidence served everyone's interests. Luistro cited time, government resources, legislative work and the budget; he argued that fairness should take priority over finishing in November and that the prosecution had chosen to spend 38 days presenting its case. He distinguished unpresented Article 3 from Articles 1 and 4, whose allegations had already reached the court and public, and asked whether their withdrawal would be treated as acquittal; Luistro began an answer but did not complete it. Describing impeachment as both judicial and political, he argued that the respondent needed an opportunity to answer accusations affecting her reputation, and asked how a Facebook broadcast or press conference could compel hostile witnesses through court subpoenas. Luistro said her alternative-platform suggestion responded to the defense's concern that prosecution evidence had already been seen publicly. In answering the Presiding Officer's hypothetical about withdrawing every article, Cayetano agreed the proceeding would then stop, but said retaining one article while withdrawing completed evidentiary accusations left the fairness problem unresolved; the Chair noted his remarks before proceeding to the ruling. In his later intervention, he appreciated the Chair's openness and colleagues' positions, urged that the defense receive a chance when trial resumed November 9, and linked the court's conduct to constitutional due process and public confidence. He questioned the lack of consultation over a whole-day November 10 sitting and reiterated that the budget should take priority, requesting discussion among Senate leaders. The Presiding Officer referred the calendar matter to the Senate President and Majority Leader.

Sen. Francis "Chiz" G. Escudero asked whether Sison was objecting to withdrawal; she clarified that she was making a manifestation reserving the defense's own decisions about presenting its case. He explored Cayetano's distinction between withdrawing every charge and withdrawing three while retaining one, asking what would happen if the prosecution abandoned all articles after presenting evidence. Cayetano agreed that complete withdrawal would end the proceeding but maintained that partial withdrawal left a fairness problem. Escudero recalled the earlier proposal to alternate prosecution and defense evidence article by article, saw the parties' and Cayetano's concerns, and urged future impeachment courts to learn from this situation. Pangilinan recalled that alternating presentation had depended on both parties agreeing, which they had not. Escudero then asked Sison what adjudicative purpose evidence on withdrawn articles would serve, distinguishing that question from the respondent's wish to be heard after the prosecution. When she questioned whether alternating articles would have changed the problem, he observed that both sides would already have been heard before a later withdrawal. In explaining his fairness concerns, he said the purpose of relaxing Rule 18 was to give the respondent an opportunity to answer, rather than create unrestricted public accusations. He returned to the complete-withdrawal hypothetical and contrasted it with the present situation, in which a surviving article kept the court and its procedures available; his closing question received no direct answer before Pangilinan spoke.
