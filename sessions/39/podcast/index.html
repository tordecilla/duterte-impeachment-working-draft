02

Prosecution chooses Article 2

The day's principal development was the prosecution's request to withdraw three of the four impeachment articles and pursue only Article 2. Lead prosecutor Representative Gerville Luistro sought withdrawal of Article 1 on confidential funds, Article 3 on bribery, and Article 4 on threats. She argued that the evidence under Article 2, concerning unexplained wealth, violations involving statements of assets, liabilities and net worth, and prohibited business interests, independently warranted the Vice President's removal.

03

The reason and the defense challenge

Luistro said narrowing the case would reduce the formal offer of evidence and the objections to it, conserve government resources, and leave the Senate time for legislation and the 2027 budget. She maintained that all four articles were equally strong. Lead defense counsel, Attorney Sheila Sison, expressly said she did not object to withdrawal itself. Her challenge concerned what followed: the prosecution could not dictate the defense's presentation or replace an opportunity to answer accusations aired in court with statements on public platforms. She warned that curtailing defense evidence to hasten judgment would bear the characteristics of a mistrial.

04

The court's withdrawal ruling

Presiding Officer Senator Francis Chiz Escudero confirmed withdrawal of Articles 1 and 4 with prejudice, and Article 3 without prejudice. Article 2 remained for trial. The bar on Articles 1 and 4 covered substantially the same constitutional accusations against the respondent during the same term and the twentieth Congress. Other proceedings governed by their own laws remained available. Article 3 stood differently because the prosecution had presented no evidentiary case under it. The withdrawn articles would receive no vote or verdict of conviction or acquittal.

05

Defense evidence and Rule 18

The court directed the respondent's evidence-in-chief toward Article 2 and allowed her to adopt relevant, otherwise admissible evidence already in the official record. In later clarification, Escudero said defense initiatives in court remained open, subject to prosecution objections and court rulings at the appropriate time. The court also relaxed Rule 18 on the separate merits of the withdrawn articles, while retaining it for Article 2. Seals, privileges, protective orders and restrictions on disclosure of evidence remained in force. The Rule 18 relaxation remained effective while the defense consulted its client and considered seeking reconsideration. Reconsideration could cover the relaxation in whole or in part, and could be acted on during recess.

06

Alan Peter Cayetano's fairness question

Senator Alan Peter Cayetano questioned how withdrawing articles after presenting their evidence served everyone's interests. He argued that fairness should take priority over finishing in November, and distinguished the unpresented Article 3 from Articles 1 and 4, whose allegations had already reached the court and the public. He asked how a Facebook broadcast or press conference could compel hostile witnesses through court subpoenas. Luistro said her suggestion of other platforms responded to the defense's concern about evidence already seen publicly. Cayetano later urged an opportunity for the defense when trial resumed, linking the court's conduct to constitutional due process and public confidence.

07

Consultation and the respondent's choice

Senator Paolo Benigno Bam Aquino asked both parties to consult their teams and the defense to consult its client, stressing the consequences of withdrawal with prejudice. He asked whose decision withdrawal was and whether reconsideration was available. Escudero answered that the prosecution requested withdrawal and the court decided it, with appeal or reconsideration available. Sison agreed to consult the client but maintained that the prosecution's decision should not preempt the respondent's choice of evidence or presentation time. Senator Risa Hontiveros said she wanted to hear the Vice President respond to the allegations. She noted that the defense had yet to tell the court how it wished to proceed. Her manifestation received no direct response before discussion moved on.

08

Proposals to keep Rule 18 in place

Senator Pia Cayetano pressed for time to consult the respondent and for a decision on Rule 18 before public discussion opened. She objected that an interim relaxation could undermine a later choice to answer in court, and proposed maintaining the status quo while the defense deliberated. Senator Francis Kiko Pangilinan also proposed keeping Rule 18 enforced pending reconsideration when trial resumed. That proposed interim continuation was not adopted. Instead, Pangilinan moved to authorize the presiding officer to act during recess, and Senator Joel Villanueva seconded. Villanueva supported most of the ruling but reserved objections to lifting Rule 18, urging adequate time for the respondent to defend herself in court even on the withdrawn articles. The relaxation remained effective pending further action.

09

The defense's remaining concerns

Sison objected to relaxing Rule 18 while Article 2 remained on trial, arguing that unrestricted public accusations could harm the integrity of the proceedings. Escudero explained that the purpose of relaxation was to give the respondent an opportunity to answer. Defense counsel and spokesperson Attorney Michael Wesley Poa raised another limit: he said a regional trial court gag order in the grave-threats case prevented the defense from speaking about Article 4 despite the impeachment court's relaxation. He also said press conferences could not substitute for subpoenaed witnesses giving controverting evidence. He undertook consultation with the respondent, hoping to report her instructions within twenty-eight to forty-eight hours.

The record and protected attachments

Withdrawal did not remove the materials from the official record. The court directed the Clerk of Court to classify attachments for public, redacted, controlled, sealed or separately protected access before release. Confidential official-use copies would carry recipient-specific watermarks and distribution records. Uploading the formal offer would not itself authorize publication of its attachments: only versions permitted under their access classification could be uploaded.

11

Earlier evidence business

Before the withdrawal debate, prosecutor Representative Jose Manuel "Chel" I. Diokno requested marking of materials associated with the previous day's witness, Attorney Alexander B. Cabrera. They included his curriculum vitae, forensic report, annexes and a flash drive. The chair directed marking later before the Clerk of Court with both parties' counsel present. Sison also sought judicial notice of the Amoroso case, arguing that piercing corporate fiction required jurisdiction and due process. Diokno responded that piercing the corporate veil was also an accounting principle and reserved further argument. The chair said legal memoranda and closing arguments would follow the respondent's evidence-in-chief.

12

What happens next

The prosecution's Article 2 formal offer was due October 23. The respondent would then have ten days from receipt to file comments and objections, followed by the court's ruling. Defense evidence-in-chief was scheduled to begin November 9. The court required five-day witness notice and requested estimates of witness numbers and trial days. The respondent's position on Rule 18 still awaited consultation and notice to the court, with the existing relaxation effective until reconsideration was acted upon. The court adjourned until November 9 at ten in the morning.
