First defense witness

Engineer Jaime G. Adalin, Davao City assessor, took the stand as the first defense witness. The central question was whether a disputed Matina Crossing property appeared in the Vice President's 2024 and 2025 statements of assets, liabilities and net worth, or SALNs. Adalin produced five tax declarations, three maps and a barangay certification. He initially identified the properties as the same, then qualified that identification under questioning about the records and other possible holdings.

The defense identification

Defense counsel Attorney Lindon Miguel C. Bacquel relied on location and monetary particulars to argue that the titled property was disclosed. Adalin used maps to locate it in Talomo District, in Matina Crossing, also called Barangay 74-A, and connect its property index number to the title ending 0775. He linked the SALN's land entry to matching assessment figures and its 2025 commercial-building entry to the building declaration. The maps and barangay certification were marked as defense exhibits.

Different values in different records

The figures came from several different fields. For the current land declaration, Adalin read an assessed value of 82,046 pesos and a market value of 1,864,680 pesos. For the building, he read roughly 5.24 million pesos assessed value and 6.99 million market value. Earlier and replacement land declarations carried the same 82,046-peso assessed value, alongside 2,051,150 pesos market value. Adalin later explained that the seller's assessed value had been carried over to Manases Carpio.

05

Prosecution challenges the match

Prosecution counsel Attorney Benjamin Tolosa Jr. challenged the identification through discrepancies. Adalin read ten million pesos acquisition cost in the 2024 SALN, against six million pesos total selling price in the eCAR. He agreed that the eCAR included an improvement while the SALN entry described a residential lot. Tolosa also pressed the absence of exact coordinates and block or lot numbers, and whether matching assessment values could identify one property uniquely. Bacquel emphasized that the SALN supplied several identifying particulars together.

Further valuation questions

Photograph identification and changing certainty

Senator Francis “Kiko” Pangilinan challenged Adalin's changing certainty about whether the SALN and deed described the same property. Adalin reaffirmed that the match rested on the eCAR. Pangilinan contrasted that assurance with his inability to identify a restaurant photograph and his renewed certainty after consulting staff. Pangilinan ended by observing that the witness had given inconsistent testimony.

Travel and credibility

Questions about China travel also tested Adalin's recollection. Senator Vicente C. Sotto III proposed checking Immigration records. Senator Erwin Tulfo reminded Adalin of his oath and warned about possible contempt; he later elicited Adalin's employment history, including his appointment as city assessor in 2017 under Sara Duterte. Under questions from Raffy Tulfo and Hontiveros, Adalin described personally funded group travel with city officials. He could not name the agency that arranged tickets and denied Sara Duterte accompanied the January trip.

What the court directed

Presiding Officer Escudero sustained the objection when Bacquel asked whether the prosecution had no basis for alleging nondisclosure. On redirect, he limited Adalin's initial acquisition-cost answer to his experience completing his own SALN and took judicial notice of specified transaction-tax rates. He directed clerk coordination on travel records. Raffy Tulfo's proposed property search and Sotto's proposed Immigration check remained proposals in those exchanges. Separately, Villanueva reported missing Cale Foods records, and Escudero reminded the Clerk to comply.
