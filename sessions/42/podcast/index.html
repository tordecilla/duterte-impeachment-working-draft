02

The central dispute

The day's defense witness was Attorney Suzette Balucanag-Punay, in-house counsel for GenCorp Industries and Metro City Chow Foods. Her testimony challenged the corporate ownership and income calculations in Attorney Alexander Cabrera's forensic report. At the center was a payment summary showing about 2.14 million pesos in disbursements to the Vice President from 2022 through 2025. Punay described those payments as returned capital. The prosecution and several senator-judges challenged that explanation and asked for the investment agreement, actual checks and accounting entries.

03

What the witness brought

Let's begin with the corporate records. Punay said GenCorp's information sheets for 2022 through 2025 accurately listed its shareholders, and that Duterte held neither shares nor a corporate position there. She disputed Cabrera's attribution of a twenty percent stake and 250,000 pesos in paid-up capital. GenCorp operated five Jollibee stores, consolidated gradually from predecessor companies. Punay traced Duterte's investment to Davao Bounty in 2007, then through successor companies to GenCorp, while remaining tied to the Times Square store. She called it a loose investment.

Payments and their documentation

The accountant's summary listed roughly 337,500 pesos in 2022, 555,750 in 2023, 612,419 in 2024 and 636,700 in 2025. Punay said the checks came from the Times Square branch account. Pangilinan: disclosure and reliability

Sen. Francis “Kiko” Pangilinan first sought corporate privilege waivers. 08

Escudero: follow the investment

Sen. Francis "Chiz" G. Escudero repeatedly returned to the chain connecting the original contribution with GenCorp's payments. He asked how the branches had transferred, what Duterte had contributed, and what obligation the successor companies were repaying. Punay had not seen the transfer documents or a written investment agreement and could not verify the amount. Escudero distinguished repayment of capital from earnings on it. In his final round, he established that GenCorp itself bid for and received government contracts, regardless of the supplying branch, and sought a documentary explanation for its payments to Duterte.
