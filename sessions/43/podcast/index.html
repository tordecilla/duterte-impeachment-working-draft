02

Two transactions at the center

Two defense witnesses gave the court different explanations for credit-card spending by Attorney Manases R. Carpio, the spouse of Vice President Sara Zimmerman Duterte. Lawyer Anel Martin S. Antero described a shared dinner in Tokyo, with friends reimbursing Carpio. Businessman Chadrin S. Parian described shoes bought for young basketball players and some accompanying adults after a championship in Malaysia. Prosecutors challenged the supporting proof and asked whether the spending met modest-living obligations. Several senator-judges questioned that emphasis and urged the parties to move to other issues. The defense presentation remained underway.

The Tokyo dinner account

Antero's testimony concerned a Ginza-Inakaya charge dated May nineteenth, twenty twenty-three: two hundred five thousand seven pesos and twenty-nine centavos. Defense counsel Attorney Lindon Miguel C. Bacquel offered the account to rebut Article Two allegations. Antero said eleven friends traveled together and ate the dinner. The bill exceeded two hundred thousand pesos, and some companions lacked enough cash for their shares. The group used Carpio's card with repayment expected. Antero recalled contributing forty thousand yen, and on cross-examination expressly affirmed that the diners reimbursed Carpio. But when prosecutors asked for corroboration, he had brought no photograph of the dinner and had no document proving repayment.

Who received the shoes

Cross-examination established that adults as well as eleven children received shoes. Parian rejected the suggested calculation of twenty-three thousand pesos per child. He said he could not afford a two-hundred-fifty-six-thousand-peso purchase himself. On redirect, he identified other recipients as parents and guardians and said not every companion chose shoes. He described families with different means and parents taking turns helping. He had contributed to an earlier discounted group purchase; on recross, he said the number of shoes was similar but could not compare the amounts, and acknowledged contributions from several people. Asked about Carpio's modest living, he said he could not answer for Carpio or know his thinking, but thought the recipients deserved the shoes. Presiding Officer Sen. Francis “Chiz” G. Escudero allowed that opinion question, then stopped repetition after his answer.

Cayetano challenges the emphasis

Senator Pia S. Cayetano warned that the questioning could discourage parents and officials from supporting young athletes. She invoked constitutional support for sports and parental duties. Asked about government assistance, Parian initially said none, then clarified that his son had received help through Palarong Pambansa. Robles agreed athletes needed support and said the prosecution had no quarrel with parental help; its concern was legally appropriate conduct by public officials. He also agreed that unbranded shoes were not required. Cayetano recounted buying shoes for her daughters' teammates and supporting overseas sports trips, including taking the University of the Philippines football team to Japan. She criticized questions revealing a child's school and warned that scrutiny could deter supporters. Her closing criticism received no further substantive prosecution response.

Escudero and Zubiri on sports support

Padilla returns to modest living

In a second round, Senator Robinhood “Robin” Padilla challenged the prosecution's simple-living argument with questions about lawmakers' expensive cars, including vehicles costing sixteen to twenty-two million pesos and a Mercedes-Benz. Robles affirmed the legal obligation and said he knew of none of the vehicles Padilla raised. Padilla urged scrutiny of other politicians' wealth and progress on Freedom of Information legislation. He contrasted the spending under discussion with his own nine-hundred-thousand-peso spending and cited assistance of three hundred thousand and three hundred forty thousand pesos to shooting victims. His concluding proposals and invitation to investigate his spending received no substantive answer.
