Atty. Anel Martin S. Antero

Defense witness; lawyer and member of the Vice President’s legal team

Antero appeared under subpoena, took the witness oath and confirmed that he understood it. After direct examination, cross-examination, redirect and questions from Sen. Imee Marcos, Presiding Officer Sen. Francis “Chiz” G. Escudero excused him.

00:29:42 · S0010900:29:54 · S00109E100:30:14 · S00112E100:31:24 · S0012701:15:32 · S00444

Lawyer and defense witness Atty. Anel Martin S. Antero testified about a shared Tokyo dinner charged to Atty. Manases R. Carpio, the spouse of Vice President Sara Zimmerman Duterte. Defense counsel Atty. Lindon Miguel C. Bacquel offered the testimony to explain the ₱205,007.29 Ginza-Inakaya charge from May 19, 2023 and rebut Article 2 allegations. Antero said eleven friends traveled together, shared a bill exceeding ₱200,000 and used Carpio’s card with repayment expected. On cross-examination he affirmed that they reimbursed Carpio; he recalled contributing ¥40,000.

00:30:30 · S0011800:32:42 · S0013900:33:13 · S00144E100:33:54 · S0015200:34:19 · S0015400:34:34 · S0015600:34:54 · S00157E100:35:06 · S0015900:35:50 · S0016701:02:55 · S0035401:03:05 · S00354E1

Under cross-examination, Antero said he had both cash and a card, while some companions lacked enough cash for their shares. Several people volunteered their cards. He brought no photograph of the dinner and had no document proving reimbursement.

The prosecution used Antero’s social-media posts to test his support for the Vice President and possible bias. Presiding Officer Sen. Francis “Chiz” G. Escudero allowed inquiry into his previously expressed views but rejected as misleading counsel’s characterization that a handcuffs post accused the issuing court of gratification.

00:42:00 · S0021900:47:51 · S0025600:48:41 · S0026100:50:02 · S00266

Antero acknowledged longstanding friendship with Duterte and Carpio, membership in the Vice President’s legal team and occasional compensation for professional work at Carpio’s firm. When prosecution counsel Atty. Amando Virgil D. Ligutan put a ₱580,000 July 2020 transaction to him from an AMLC report, Antero said he did not recall it. On redirect he said he would not lie for either spouse.

Answering senator-judge Sen. Imee Marcos, Antero said Carpio wanted credit-card points and that the friends had shared bills before. He said Duterte was absent from the dinner and he did not know whether she knew of the transaction.

02

Chadrin S. Parian

Defense witness; businessman and parent of an Elite Philippines basketball player

Parian was called to the stand, took the witness oath and confirmed that he understood it. He underwent direct examination, cross-examination, redirect, recross and senator-judge questions before Presiding Officer Sen. Francis “Chiz” G. Escudero excused him.

01:17:23 · S0045301:17:54 · S0045601:18:37 · S0046502:20:52 · S00988

Businessman and defense witness Chadrin S. Parian, the parent of an Elite Philippines basketball player, testified about shoes bought after the children’s team won a championship in Malaysia. Defense counsel Atty. Justin Nicol B. Gular offered his testimony to explain the ₱256,882.50 Nike Pavilion Kuala Lumpur charge and rebut Article 2 allegations. Parian said he saw the children choose shoes and Carpio pay near the counter, and photographed the purchase.

01:18:42 · S0046701:21:04 · S0048401:21:16 · S0048601:24:50 · S0051701:25:38 · S0052801:27:18 · S0054501:27:41 · S0055001:27:45 · S00550_M301:27:54 · S00551_M201:29:46 · S00563_M201:40:27 · S00688

Parian supplied tickets, Philippine arrival and departure records, Malaysian immigration material, photographs and a tournament invitation. Prosecution counsel Atty. Reynaldo Robles stipulated to authenticity and due execution of the travel documents, Carpio’s and the team’s presence, and authentication of the photographs and documents; he also agreed to the invitation after clarification. The defense identified the material for later marking as Exhibit D-2-1-Parian and series.

Parian agreed on cross-examination that adults as well as the eleven children received shoes. He rejected the proposed ₱23,000-per-child calculation and said he could not afford a ₱256,000 purchase himself. On redirect he identified other recipients as parents and guardians, said not every companion selected shoes, and described families with different means and parents taking turns to help.

Parian said he had contributed to an earlier group purchase of discounted shoes. On recross he said the number of shoes was similar but could not compare the amounts, and agreed that the earlier purchase involved contributions from several people. Asked about Carpio’s modest living, Parian said he could not answer for Carpio or know his thinking, but thought the children and some parents deserved the shoes.
