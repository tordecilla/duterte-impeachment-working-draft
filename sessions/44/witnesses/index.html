Atty. Rainer P. Dela Fuente

Defense witness; certified public accountant, lawyer and former AMLC financial investigator

Atty. Rainer P. Dela Fuente testified for the defense on anti-money-laundering standards and financial-analysis methods. The prosecution and senator-judges challenged opinions offered without review of the complete forensic report or underlying records. Dela Fuente cited confidentiality and his lack of authority to access the records, and confined his assessment to displayed excerpts and hypothetical questions. Presiding Officer Sen. Francis "Chiz" G. Escudero reminded counsel that Dela Fuente had not been qualified as a constitutional-law expert and directed counsel to wind up that line or move on.

Dela Fuente took the oath, then described his accounting, legal and AMLC background and identified his professional profile, financial-investigation handbook, 2012 FATF guidance report and GoTRACS transaction-reporting guidelines. The defense requested exhibit marking. The prosecution declined to stipulate to his expertise; the court allowed opinion testimony subject to cross-examination.

Dela Fuente described covered transaction reports (CTRs) and suspicious transaction reports (STRs) as indicators requiring verification, with bank inquiries supplying the underlying transaction records. He distinguished inflows from income, returned capital from earnings, and corporate receipts from shareholder income. On cross-examination, he agreed that a forensic accountant could use corporate-veil analysis in a professional report.

Dela Fuente said AMLC information retains its confidentiality and that authorized disclosure requires safeguards. In later questioning, he agreed that an impeachment-court order allowing review or compelling production would constitute authorized disclosure.

Dela Fuente said he was not alleging that the AMLC, bank or other government records were fabricated or untruthful. He agreed that an unexplained-wealth case could proceed without a money-laundering finding if the evidence established unexplained wealth.

Dela Fuente agreed that the Cale88 patterns described during questioning were red flags warranting investigation. He identified contracts, invoices and shipment records as ways to verify claimed export payments, and said credible information about a foreign criminal investigation could justify enhanced due diligence and suspicious-transaction reporting.

The court clarified that Article 2 concerned unexplained or ill-gotten wealth rather than a separate AMLA offense, while allowing the defense to challenge the AMLC evidence supporting the charge. Dela Fuente completed direct examination, cross-examination, redirect, recross and senator questions and was excused. The court scheduled three further defense witnesses for November 20 at 10 a.m.
