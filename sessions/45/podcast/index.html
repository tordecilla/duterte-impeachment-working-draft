Agency records and financial questions

Registration and incentives

Defense witness Dr. Ceferino S. Rodolfo, DTI Undersecretary and BOI Vice Chairman and Managing Head, said CALE88 Foods Corporation registered for banana-chip exports in December twenty twenty-one and reported starting commercial operations in January twenty twenty-three. Its reports listed direct labor of one hundred fifteen, two hundred one, and fifty across twenty twenty-three through twenty twenty-five. BOI had no recorded cancellation of registration or suspension of incentives. Senator Imee Marcos asked whether agency records indicated fictitious or inflated operations. Rodolfo said no, citing reports and an inspection that found operations. He described VAT zero rating used in twenty twenty-four and twenty twenty-six, covering performance in twenty twenty-three and twenty twenty-five. The company had not used an income-tax holiday. In a later round, Marcos raised banana-sector problems and Panama disease. Rodolfo confirmed the disease and offered early losses and third-year net income of three hundred seventy-five thousand pesos as a possible explanation for not seeking the holiday.

Checks behind the paperwork

Prosecution counsel Attorney Erwin G. Matib challenged late notarization, amended reports, conflicting employment figures and missing labor-benefit records. Defense witness Dr. Ceferino S. Rodolfo, DTI Undersecretary and BOI Vice Chairman and Managing Head, said BOI relied on company submissions and had not conducted the labor checks associated with an income-tax-holiday application. He said BOI evaluated a twelve-million-peso project plan and its proposed capacity; he could not judge the growth Tulfo outlined.

Workers and physical verification

Senator Joel Villanueva questioned CALE88 Foods Corporation's inconsistent employment entries, labor standards and inspection gap. Defense witness Dr. Ceferino S. Rodolfo, DTI Undersecretary and BOI Vice Chairman and Managing Head, said the fifty-worker figure came from a notarized report checked against financial statements, without a physical headcount. The pre-registration inspection was virtual; a good-standing request and reported employment decline triggered the next inspection in twenty twenty-six. Villanueva also asked about Chinese counterparties and remittances; Rodolfo said BOI's ordinary requirements did not identify state ownership. Villanueva cited approximately one hundred ninety-four million pesos in Chinese remittances. Rodolfo said that, if correct, those receipts represented most of the roughly two hundred nine million pesos in three-year sales reported to BOI. Senator Raffy T. Tulfo separately cited a report listing four hundred twenty-three employees against BOI's direct-labor figure of two hundred one. He also asked how BOI verified exports. Rodolfo said it had no such monitoring and relied on notarized submissions and bank certifications. Tulfo proposed checking Bureau of Customs records. He proposed checking PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG and SSS records. Rodolfo said income-tax-holiday applications required SSS documentation and agreed it was possible the company did not have two hundred one employees.

Sales, bank receipts and export proof

Senator Sherwin T. Gatchalian compared roughly seven hundred seventy-six million pesos in Anti-Money Laundering Council inflows with about two hundred eight million pesos in reported sales, asking whether sales were understated. Tax treatment and competing accounts

Senator Vicente C. Sotto III questioned whether CALE88 Foods Corporation had continuously benefited from incentives. Defense witness Dr. Ceferino S. Rodolfo, DTI Undersecretary and BOI Vice Chairman and Managing Head, distinguished its two years of VAT zero rating from the unused income-tax holiday and duty-free importation. Asked about inflows far exceeding sales, he said CALE88 had declared no other income source to BOI and explaining the excess would be guessing. He pointed to company officers and former part-owner and treasurer Dr. Lourdes Lebosada as possible sources of explanation. Aquino proposed calling the people involved; the chair moved to the next senator.
