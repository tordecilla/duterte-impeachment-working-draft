Senator-judge interventions

Sen. Panfilo "Ping" M. Lacson contrasted the NBI's handling of similar alleged threats by Mayor Sebastian Duterte and Vice President Sara Duterte. He asked whether Mayor Duterte was under active investigation, whether the Special Task Force began with his case or the Vice President's, and why the NBI used Mayor Duterte's statements as open-source material while pursuing a DOJ complaint against the Vice President; Lacson said the disparity could suggest a double standard. NBI Director Melvin Matibag said the Mayor Duterte incident had triggered the task force within a broader threat assessment and that the NBI was still building a possible conspiracy case. Lacson also tested the use of Arturo Lascañas's ICC affidavit, asking whether it had evidentiary value and why the NBI's open-source review had not accounted for Lascañas's Senate reversals and multiple affidavits. Matibag said the affidavit was a reference for a pattern-of-violence analysis, not direct criminal proof; the bureau knew of the reversals, compared the document with Lascañas's public account of it, and noted that the ICC was considering it.

Sen. Alan Peter Cayetano asked whether the NBI had ultimately accepted Arturo Lascañas's affidavit after Director Melvin Matibag said it was not believed immediately. Matibag said the agency later relied on the affidavit in its pattern-of-violence analysis after its investigative process, clarifying the document's place in the NBI theory.

Risa Hontiveros asked whether Vice President Sara Duterte had answered, in any way, allegations in Arturo Lascañas's affidavit, which NBI Director Melvin Matibag said had been reviewed as part of the wider inquiry into her capacity to carry out the alleged threat. Matibag said the bureau considered open-source reports as well as the affidavit, and recalled reports that Duterte had said she did not need the DDS to do what it could do. Asked to specify that reported statement, he supplied the latter phrase but said he could not recall the exact wording.

Erwin Tulfo asked NBI Director Melvin Matibag how investigators identified the persons of interest whom Vice President Sara Duterte had allegedly asked to kill the President, First Lady, and former House Speaker, when her press conference did not name them. Matibag said the NBI combined open-source and human intelligence with monitoring tools, then narrowed a larger pool. Asked what could be said about the individuals without identifying them, Matibag described examining Duterte's networks, including people in uniform, the Vice Presidential Security Group, and the Davao Death Squad, and said the pool had been reduced to a few personalities. Tulfo then asked whether the threat remained capable of execution and whether the President and PSG had been notified. Matibag called it an active, continuing threat because the people approached had not been identified, and said the NBI maintained ongoing coordination and threat assessments with the Palace.

01:09:03 · S0057701:10:22 · S0059701:12:30 · S0060201:13:13 · S00604

Sen. Raffy T. Tulfo compared the earlier arrests of Ronel Mas, Ronald Quiboyen, Maria Catherine Seron, and Joe Mark Ebkay with the NBI's handling of alleged threats by Vice President Sara Duterte and Mayor Sebastian Duterte. After Director Melvin Matibag described the earlier cases as PNP-CIDG arrests involving alleged offers of rewards to kill former President Rodrigo Duterte, Tulfo noted that the accused were ordinary citizens and asked why law enforcement had used what he called a heavy hand against them while treating powerful figures differently. Matibag said he could not speak for the earlier officers, acknowledged that the warrantless arrests had led to the cases' dismissal, and said the present investigation was following case build-up and court process. Asked who had ordered the earlier arrests, Matibag did not identify an order and instead said that evidence, law, and process should govern. Tulfo then used a hypothetical involving Leni Robredo and President Duterte to ask whether authorities would have acted just as quickly; Matibag said that outcome was possible.

01:25:38 · S0075201:26:31 · S00759C01:26:57 · S00759E01:27:06 · S0076401:28:15 · S0083401:28:49 · S00840

Sen. Joel Villanueva asked NBI Director Melvin Matibag whether the investigation's projected three-to-six-month development meant it was now verifying the identity of the person whom Vice President Sara Duterte said she had approached, rather than determining whether such a person existed. Matibag called Duterte's video statement an extrajudicial admission that someone had been approached; he said the inquiry was directed at identifying that person and assessing the threat to national security, not treating an unexecuted discussion alone as a crime. Villanueva also asked whether current information about the Davao Death Squad, rather than historical background alone, informed that assessment. NBI Director Melvin Matibag said the division was included to examine the people involved, their capacity, funding, and network, as well as threats to the President during foreign travel. Aquino pressed whether its inclusion instead reflected possible foreign intervention in the plot; Matibag said that remained an angle under consideration, but not the division's main purpose.

01:35:49 · S0086801:37:48 · S00891

Sen. Robinhood "Robin" Padilla challenged the prosecution over contradictions he said appeared in Arturo Lascañas's earlier statements and affidavit. Lead prosecutor Gerville Luistro resisted giving the requested label and agreed, in the Chair's formulation, that a witness whose accounts change may be unreliable. Padilla then asked the prosecution to avoid presenting witnesses with pending perjury cases, naming Jeremy Lotoc and NBI Agent Calilung. Luistro said its verification found no pending cases against the prosecution witnesses and added that a conviction disqualifies a witness only if final, punishable by more than a year, and involving moral turpitude. Padilla also asked why the ICC had been raised; Luistro said it appeared in the Lascañas affidavit that the prosecution offered as part of the testimony and NBI special-task-force investigation. Turning to Director Melvin Matibag, Padilla probed his political work and past relationship with Padilla; Matibag acknowledged helping candidates and parties but said he was Mar Roxas's Region IV-A campaign manager in 2016, not an architect of Rodrigo Duterte's victory.

Francis "Kiko" Pangilinan asked NBI Director Melvin Matibag whether the agency had coordinated with other government bodies after treating the alleged threat as a national-security concern, and which agencies were involved. Matibag described continuing coordination through the security cluster, the Office of the Executive Secretary, and military units, while declining to name the specific unit. Pangilinan then tested the opposite hypothetical: Matibag said the NBI had monitored no retaliatory threat by the President and that retaliation would not justify a threat; self-defense, he added, depends on its legal elements and an actual danger. The exchange separated interagency response from whether a perceived threat could excuse a counter-threat.

Sen. Christopher Bong Go addressed the 2011 confrontation between then-Mayor Sara Duterte and a sheriff, which had been raised during the defense cross-examination. He asked NBI Director Melvin Matibag whether a fair investigation must consider the context of a person's actions; Matibag said historical behavior could be used to establish a pattern of violence. Go then asked about the demolition affecting 217 families and the city's flood aftermath. Matibag said the sheriff was carrying a legal demolition order and that he had only been informed that day of the asserted surrounding circumstances. Go gave his personal recollection that Duterte had asked for a short delay so residents without relocation could remain, arguing that this episode differed from the acts under investigation and should be assessed in context.

Imee Marcos asked NBI Director Melvin Matibag to reconcile the bureau's earlier statements that it had leads and findings when it filed grave-threat and inciting-to-sedition complaints against Vice President Sara Duterte with its continuing inability, 14 months later, to identify the alleged assassin. Matibag said an identified assassin was not an element of those complaints: the NBI considered their elements complete, while separately investigating what it called an active national-security threat. Marcos challenged whether that distinction squared with the House testimony; Matibag maintained that the earlier reference was to leads and persons of interest, not an identified assassin. She then asked why Administrative Order No. 11 and a special task force were needed, including whether the Rage Coalition event supported an alleged conspiracy between Duterte and Mayor Baste. Matibag said Duterte neither attended nor spoke at that event, but the NBI was examining a possible conspiracy and still needed to build a case. Marcos questioned what the task force could add while impeachment was under way and asked whether Matibag could return after the stated three-to-six-month investigation. Matibag did not address that request; instead, he said impeachment did not require a criminal conviction, while the NBI still had to identify the people involved and determine the threat's full scope for the criminal inquiry.

Sen. Loren Legarda asked NBI Director Melvin Matibag whether the NBI had coordinated with other agencies over the alleged threats and whether that coordination was documented.
