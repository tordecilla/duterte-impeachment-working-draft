## Introduction

This is Working Draft's audio overview of the Impeachment Trial of Vice President Sara Duterte for July 20, 2026. This is a working reference, not an official Senate record.

## The big picture

The sitting centered on prosecution requests for bank, Anti-Money Laundering Council, and tax records tied to Article 2. The impeachment court granted substantial parts of the requests, but the records were not yet evidence and the orders imposed significant limits.

## The parties' positions

The prosecution argued that the records were definite and relevant and that production would not decide admissibility. The defense opposed issuance, calling the requests intrusive, overbroad, legally restricted in part, and a fishing expedition.

## The bank and AMLC order

The court found the specified records reasonably described and prima facie relevant. It allowed peso-account requests involving the Vice President, her spouse, Carpio Lawyers, and nineteen linked entities, while denying requests for two corporations that lacked a sufficient prima facie connection at that stage.

## Safeguards and limits

Foreign-currency deposits were excluded without each depositor's written consent. Anti-Money Laundering Council material had to be submitted in camera, document-specific privilege claims remained available, and the chair removed catch-all language so only listed documents were covered.

## The tax-record order

The Bureau of Internal Revenue subpoena was only a preliminary step. The commissioner was directed to make a lawful return on July 30, and the court said it would not receive tax records unless a statutory basis for disclosure was satisfactorily shown.

## What the orders did not decide

No witness testified and no subpoenaed bank, Anti-Money Laundering Council, or tax record was admitted that day. The older records, from 2007 through 2021, were authorized only as a comparative baseline tied to the current Article 2 allegation, not as proof of another impeachable offense.
