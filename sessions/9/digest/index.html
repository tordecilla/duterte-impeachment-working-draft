Senator-judge interventions

Sen. Robinhood "Robin" Padilla questioned whether the detention of Senators Jinggoy Estrada and Rodante Marcoleta, Ombudsman communications to him and Alan Peter Cayetano, and the announced SEA Games inquiry after Cayetano questioned the NBI director were coincidence or a pattern of pressure on the court. He asked whether senator-judges should fear those developments and stressed that the impeachment court had independent constitutional authority to conduct the trial.

00:06:51 · S00028

Sen. Alan Peter Cayetano said the announced SEA Games inquiry could chill senator-judges; he denied that its references to a no-bid contract and congressional insertion implicated him, later clarified that the cauldron was privately funded, and did not oppose an investigation. On the subpoena motions, he urged separate treatment of the distinct record, account-holder, currency, and company issues; sought adequate rebuttal time and, before caucus, asked whether the parties could be questioned on those issues and whether witnesses would appear Monday. He also supported limiting withdrawal manifestations because explanations could put untested assertions before the court without a witness or opportunity to object, and said the parties could explain withdrawals in press briefings.

04

Prosecution position

The prosecution asked for bank records, tax records, and AMLC reports concerning Duterte and her husband, Manases Carpio, saying the material related to Article 2 allegations of unexplained wealth, undeclared assets, and prohibited business participation while she was Vice President.

00:41:57 · S00149

Diokno argued that the Constitution gives the Impeachment Court broad authority to issue subpoenas and that neither the Bank Secrecy Law nor the Data Privacy Act barred the requested production.

00:44:01 · S00151

The prosecution characterized Duterte's financial activity from 2007 through 2013, as described in an AMLC report submitted to the House committee, as relevant to whether she was fit to remain Vice President. This was the prosecution's argument about the report, not a finding by the impeachment court.

00:45:40 · S0015200:47:15 · S00153

Diokno invoked the Corona impeachment and Supreme Court language about the Legislature's discretion over impeachable offenses to argue that pre-vice-presidential records could be examined and that the court itself should decide relevance.

00:49:25 · S0015400:51:59 · S0015500:53:11 · S00156

In rebuttal, Diokno argued that issuing subpoenas would not itself deny due process because the defense could object when particular documents were later identified and offered. He also cited Republic v. Rabusa and disputed the defense's interpretation of the confidentiality statutes.

05

Defense position

Poa agreed that the court had subpoena power but argued that a valid subpoena still had to be definite and relevant. He characterized an oppressive or unreasonable request made in the hope of uncovering incriminating material as a fishing expedition.

00:55:44 · S00162

The defense argued that the original House complaints contained no financial transaction record or SALN supporting the unexplained-wealth accusation and that the later effort to obtain records was an attempt to find proof after making the allegation.

00:57:48 · S0018500:59:52 · S00186

Citing Duterte v. House of Representatives, Poa argued that conduct from 2007, when Duterte was not yet in an impeachable office, fell outside the proper scope of the trial and made the nearly two-decade request overbroad.

01:00:29 · S00208

The defense maintained that AMLC and tax-disclosure statutes did not contain an impeachment exception, and that the Bank Secrecy Law's impeachment exception did not eliminate due process or automatically extend to the bank records of Duterte's spouse.

01:03:09 · S0020901:04:11 · S0021001:04:40 · S00211

In surrebuttal, Poa argued that due process applied at every stage, that a subpoena should not be used to suspend statutory protections, and that the Corona impeachment predated the Supreme Court guidance on which the defense relied.
